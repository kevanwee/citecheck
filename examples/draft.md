# Submissions on implied terms

The test for implying a term in fact is the three-step framework set out in *Sembcorp Marine
Ltd v PPL Holdings Pte Ltd* [2013] SGCA 43 at [101]. The court must first identify a true
gap in the contract, then ask whether it is necessary in the business or commercial sense to
imply the term, and finally apply the officious bystander test.

That framework was reaffirmed in *Ang Tin Yong v Ang Boon Chye* [2011] SGCA 60 and more
recently in [2024] SGHC 987 at [44], where the High Court declined to imply a term of good
faith into a distribution agreement.

In England the approach is now governed by *Marks and Spencer plc v BNP Paribas Securities
Services Trust Co (Jersey) Ltd* [2015] UKSC 72, [2016] AC 742.

The court's power to grant relief is found in s 18(2) of the Supreme Court of Judicature Act
1969, read with the First Schedule. See also section 2 of the Interpretation Act 1965 on
computation of months, and ss 6(1) and 24A of the Limitation Act 1959.

The Sembcorp framework ([2013] SGCA 43) remains good law.
